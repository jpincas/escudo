# Telegram onboarding

Two minutes per person, sitting next to them, on their own phone. Never by
instructions on paper.

<figure class="escudo-diagram">
  <img src="/img/diagrams/en/p-telegram-1200.png" alt="Four steps: join the group, open the button once, make it audible, look but don't press.">
</figure>

## This never stops

The first sign-up meeting is the biggest batch, not the job. **Onboarding is a
standing task**, and it belongs to the
[Assistants]({{< relref "/how-it-works/the-red-escudo" >}}). It comes round
whenever:

- somebody moves in, turns 18, or finally says yes after six months of watching;
- **somebody gets a new phone** — see below, this is the big one;
- somebody comes back for the summer, or is here for a season;
- somebody reinstalls Telegram, or clears out their chats.

A village that onboards once and considers it done has a member list that
quietly rots.

## Nobody presses the button to test it

Say this out loud, because the instinct is exactly wrong.

**A press alarms the whole village, every time.** Twenty people at a meeting each
having a go is twenty alarms in an hour. Do it casually as people join through
the year and the village gets an alarm every few days for no reason — and then
stops reacting. A village that has taught itself to ignore the alarm is worse off
than one with no alarm at all.

**Test alerts belong to announced [drills]({{< relref "testing-and-false-alarms" >}}),
and to nobody else.**

## The four steps

1. **Join the group** `Red Escudo <Village>`. Somebody already in it adds them.
2. **Open Botón Escudo and press START, once.** It's the one big button in an
   otherwise empty chat. Press it and the alert buttons appear, and stay there
   for good.
3. **Turn notifications on, for the group and for the button.** Off silent, off
   vibrate-only. This is the step everything else rests on.
4. **Look at the buttons. Don't press one.** What they need to leave with is
   knowing where the button lives and that it never moves.

Then tick them off the list.

## Proving the phone makes a noise

You still have to check step 3, and you can, without alarming anyone.

**An alert arrives as an ordinary message in the group.** So any message in the
group tests exactly the same notification. Type "prueba" and watch.

- **At a sign-up session:** once, at the end, with everyone still sitting there.
  Every phone in the room should make a noise; fix the ones that don't before
  anybody leaves. That is the whole value of the session.
- **For one person, any Tuesday:** post a message in the group, or send them one
  directly. No alert, no drill, nobody disturbed.

## What goes wrong

- **A new phone silently undoes all of it.** The most common failure by far.
  Telegram comes back with their chats, so they look fine — but notifications are
  back to default, and often the button chat is gone. Anyone who mentions a new
  phone gets onboarded again, from step 1.
- **They join the group but never open the button.** Invisible, because they
  still *receive* every alert through the group and seem perfectly signed up.
  What they can't do is **raise** one — and they're usually the person who most
  needs to.
- **The phone has been on silent since March.** Also invisible. Test, don't ask.
- **They clear their chats and the buttons vanish.** They come back by opening
  Botón Escudo and typing anything at all.
- **Somebody presses it "to see what happens".** Expect it. It's a false alarm,
  cancelled like any other, and nobody is told off —
  [no exceptions]({{< relref "testing-and-false-alarms" >}}). Just don't build it
  into the procedure.
- **The phone is genuinely too old, or there isn't one.** Don't fudge it. Note
  the name — that's who
  [phones and devices]({{< relref "onboarding-physical-buttons" >}}) are for, and
  meanwhile they're on the phone tree.

Setting up the group and the software in the first place is a different job,
belonging to the [Technical lead]({{< relref "/how-it-works/the-red-escudo" >}})
and [written out in full]({{< relref "/technology/telegram/setting-it-up" >}}).
