# Protocols

Five short procedures. Between them they cover starting a Red Escudo, getting
people onto it, keeping it working, and what to do when the alarm goes off.

They're deliberately thin. A protocol nobody can remember is decoration.

- **[Introducing the scheme]({{< relref "introducing-the-scheme" >}})** — the
  order you talk to people in, and why you don't start at the puesto.
- **[Telegram onboarding]({{< relref "onboarding-telegram" >}})** — the four
  things each neighbour does, at a table, with their own phone.
- **[Phone and device onboarding]({{< relref "onboarding-physical-buttons" >}})**
  — for people a smartphone can't reach: the phone they already own, or a
  pendant the family buys.
- **[Response]({{< relref "response" >}})** — what you do when an alert comes in,
  and the short list of things you never do.
- **[Testing and false alarms]({{< relref "testing-and-false-alarms" >}})** —
  drills twice a year, and why a false alarm is a good sign.

Checking on neighbours who live alone is the other half of what a Red Escudo is
for, and villages do it their own way — so there's no protocol here telling them
how. What matters to Escudo is the tripwire: **nobody answering is itself a
reason to raise an alert.**

## The three that aren't negotiable

Everything else here is a village's to change. These three aren't, because they
are what keeps Escudo legal and worth belonging to:

1. **Call 062 or 112 first.** Escudo supplements the authorities. It never
   replaces or bypasses them.
2. **Observe, report, help — never intervene, pursue or detain.**
3. **Behaviour and vehicles, never ethnicity or origin.** No photographs of
   people, ever.
