# The Red Escudo

The network is the people. Everything else — the button, the signs, the software
— is scaffolding around a list of neighbours who said yes in advance.

**Yes, wake me. Yes, I'll go if I can.**

That's the whole commitment, and it's deliberately small. A member is not on
duty, not rostered, and not obliged to answer any particular alert. Declining is
a normal answer; the network works because *someone* answers, not because
everyone does.

## Who's in it

Anyone who lives in the village and is over 18. Not only the fit and the young:

- The neighbour who can be at any house in four minutes.
- The one who can't run anywhere but is home all day and answers the phone.
- The one with the tractor, the water bowser, the ladder, the 4×4.
- The one who is a nurse, a first-aider, a retired firefighter.
- **The people the network exists to reach.** Anyone can raise an alert;
  everyone gets the button. There is
  [no separate list of the vulnerable]({{< relref "/philosophy/dignity-and-inclusion" >}}).

Around fifteen to fifty people works for a village of a couple of hundred. Fewer
than ten and a summer weekend empties the network; many more than fifty and the
group gets noisy and people mute it.

## Joining

At a sign-up meeting, with phones in hand — never by leaflet. Two minutes per
person, and it never stops: people move in, say yes late, and above all
[change their phone]({{< relref "/protocols/onboarding-telegram" >}}).

1. Join the group, and open Botón Escudo once so the buttons appear. **Look at
   them; don't press one** — every press alarms the whole village.
2. **Set notifications to make a noise, and prove it before they leave.** A
   silenced phone is the single biggest failure mode in this whole system, and an
   ordinary message in the group tests it without alarming anyone.
3. Read the [Response protocol]({{< relref "/protocols/response" >}}). It is the one page every member should have read.

## The jobs somebody has to hold

Not ranks, and not authority over anyone. Jobs, named out loud so they don't
quietly become nobody's.

<figure class="escudo-diagram">
  <img src="/img/diagrams/en/roles-1200.png" alt="Three roles: one coordinator, one or two assistants, and one technical lead.">
</figure>

### The Coordinator — one person

The Red Escudo is somebody's responsibility or it is nobody's. One named person
holds the whole thing:

- **Owns the member list** and knows who is actually in the network this month.
- **Is the known face** for the Junta Vecinal and the Ayuntamiento. Not a
  spokesman for the village — a phone number that gets answered.
- **Closes off incidents** in the group, and calls the drills.
- **Decides the boring things** so they get decided.

One, not a committee. A committee of three postpones; one person with two helpers
acts. But name a **deputy** who can do the job for a fortnight in August.

### Assistants — one or two

The Coordinator's hands, and the reason sign-ups actually finish:

- Sit next to neighbours at sign-up and get the phone working —
  [that's most of the work]({{< relref "/technology/telegram/setting-it-up" >}}).
- Chase the people who joined the group but never pressed Start, and the ones
  whose phone has been on silent since March.
- Take the list, the drills and the sign-ups off the Coordinator when needed.

### The Technical lead — one person

Deliberately separate, because it is a different skill and it is the one role
where the village needs somebody who genuinely enjoys this:

- **Sets up and runs the Telegram bot and the group.** Holds the bot's token —
  the only password in the system.
- **Runs whatever the bot runs on**, and keeps it running.
- **Registers phones and devices** as households ask, and keeps the
  [test rota]({{< relref "/technology/devices" >}}) that proves they still work.
- **Hands over cleanly.** The token, the config file and the accounts written
  down somewhere the Coordinator can reach them. A village that can't replace
  this person hasn't finished setting up.

This is the role most likely to be a son, a daughter or a grandchild, and it does
not require living in the village.

## Keeping it alive

A network nobody has used in a year isn't a network. Twice a year is enough:

- **A drill.** Someone raises a test alert. Count how many phones made a noise
  and how long the first neighbour took. That number is the health of the Red.
- **A review of the list.** People move, die, fall out, change phones.

If the answer to *how long did the first neighbour take* is getting worse, fix
notifications before anything else.
