# How it works

Escudo is four things: **people who agreed in advance, a button that reaches them
all at once, rules for what happens next, and signs saying so.** This section is
the first three, end to end.

## Two words, used precisely

Everything below turns on two terms. They mean one thing each, everywhere on this
site.

**The Red Escudo** is *the people* — the neighbours who have joined. Not an app,
not an organisation, not a building.

**The button** is *the thing you press to raise the alarm*. There is one button
in the model. It exists in two forms, because not everybody can use a phone.

  <img src="/img/diagrams/en/the-button-1200.png" alt="One button, two forms: the virtual button in Telegram, which asks Fire, Medical, Crime or Emergency; and a physical button on a wall or worn, with one press and no options. Both produce the same alert into the same group.">
</figure>

The **virtual button** is for anyone with a phone —
[built and in use]({{< relref "/technology/telegram" >}}). A **physical button**
is for anyone who can't work a phone in a crisis: a pendant, a watch, a unit on
the kitchen wall, or simply
[the phone they already own]({{< relref "/technology/devices" >}}). The form is a
detail of the person, not of the system.

Which is why the categories are **options on one button, not a row of buttons.**
A neighbour has one thing to remember: *press it.*

Four of those options are emergencies and wake the whole village. One —
*[pedir ayuda]({{< relref "types-of-emergency" >}})* — arrives silently, for the
things that are a real need but not an emergency.

## The whole model in eight lines

1. Neighbours join the **Red Escudo**. Joining means saying yes to two things:
   *wake me*, and *I'll go if I can*.
2. Something happens. Whoever notices it — the person in trouble, a neighbour, a
   passer-by — **presses the button**, in whichever form they have.
3. **Call 062 or 112 first**, always, if the situation needs them. The button
   does not replace the call.
4. The alert reaches **every member at once**: what kind, who raised it, when,
   and a location if it was shared.
5. Whoever can go, **says so in the group** — so the rest know it's covered and
   nobody arrives to find eleven people in a kitchen.
6. They go, and they **observe, report and help. They never intervene, pursue or
   detain.**
7. False alarm? Press *False alarm*. Nobody is told off. That's the price of
   people pressing the button when it's real.
8. Someone closes it off in the group afterwards, in one line.
