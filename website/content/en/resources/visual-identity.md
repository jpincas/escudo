# Visual identity

Escudo's identity is deliberately sober: one crest, two typefaces and a short palette. It should read as **serious and trustworthy** — close to the institutional, but warm — and never as police-like or aggressive. These are the pieces and the few rules for using them well.

## The crest

The symbol is a **quartered shield** with a double border. On top, the people: an **elder with a cane** and a **figure sheltered inside a shield**. Below, the things the community responds to: a **medical cross** and a **flame**. Together they sum up the model: neighbours looking after one another in an emergency, whether health, fire or otherwise.

The icons are from [Material Design Icons](https://pictogrammers.com/library/mdi/) (Apache-2.0). The crest is drawn by a Python generator, not traced by hand: to change it you edit the script, never move the paths of the resulting SVG.

## Variants

Six versions cover any background and any printing technique: in **green**, **amber** or **black and white**, each in a **positive** version (for light backgrounds) and a **reversed** version (for dark backgrounds).

**Green** is the brand colour; **amber** provides the accent (alerts, headers, highlights). **Paper** and **ink** are the background and the text; **stone** is for secondary text and details.

**Alarm red** is for the alarm itself: the SOS button, a live alert, a danger warning. Use it sparingly — put next to things that aren't emergencies, it stops meaning emergency. Amber is the everyday accent.

## Typography

- **Bricolage Grotesque** — for headlines, the wordmark and signage. It's the brand's voice with character.
- **Public Sans** — for body text. Neutral, legible and freely licensed.

Both are open-source typefaces, so anyone can reproduce the materials at no licensing cost.

## Correct use

- Always leave **room around the crest**; don't crowd it against other elements or box it in tightly.
- Don't change its colours, distort it or redraw it by hand: use one of the six variants.
- On dark backgrounds or photographs, use the **reversed** version; on light backgrounds, the **positive** one.
- The crest represents the whole community. Don't pair it with the language of patrols, armed vigilance or singling out people.

## Telegram icons

The two elements of the [alert layer]({{< relref "/technology/telegram" >}}) have
their own circular icon. They are not the crest: below about 128px the
quartering and the four figures turn to mush, and Telegram shows them at around
64px. They keep the shield silhouette and the palette, and nothing else.
