# Examples around the world

None of this is new. Neighbours have been organising themselves for decades, in
countries with the same problems and much better data than we have. Three schemes
are worth studying: one for its longevity, one because it's the closest relative
of what we're building, and one because it proves the mechanism works.

- **[Neighbourhood Watch]({{< relref "neighbourhood-watch" >}})** (UK, 1982) —
  1.4 million households, run by residents, supported by the police but never
  owned by them. The governance model.
- **[Buurtpreventie and SAAR]({{< relref "buurtpreventie-saar" >}})**
  (Netherlands, 2015) — thousands of neighbourhood WhatsApp groups sharing one
  four-step protocol. The source of our rules.
- **[GoodSAM]({{< relref "goodsam" >}})** (UK, Australia and beyond) — ambulance
  services dispatching nearby volunteers to cardiac arrests. Proof that alerting
  the nearest willing person saves lives.

And the part most schemes skip:
**[what the evidence actually says]({{< relref "evidence" >}})**, including the
studies that found no effect and the ones that found participants feeling
*worse*.

## What Escudo takes from each

| | Take | Leave |
|---|---|---|
| **Neighbourhood Watch** | Residents own it, police support it, signage is visible | Crime-only framing; passive membership |
| **Buurtpreventie** | The SAAR protocol, written house rules, low message volume | Unmoderated chat; suspicion as the main activity |
| **GoodSAM** | Alert the nearest volunteers, who accept or decline | Central dispatch, training requirements, an app to install |

There is no Spanish Neighbourhood Watch — policing is a state function and
citizen patrols have no legal cover. What exists is **collaboration**: the
Guardia Civil's *Plan Mayor Seguridad* and *Plan Agradecidos*, aimed squarely at
isolated rural elders, plus the free **AlertCops** app and subsidised
**teleasistencia**. Escudo layers on top of all of it, and
[never around it]({{< relref "/philosophy/horizontal-not-top-down" >}}).
