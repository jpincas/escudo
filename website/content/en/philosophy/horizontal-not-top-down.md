# Horizontal, not top-down

Almost every safety system offered to a village points away from it: press the
button and the alert leaves, to a control room, a dispatcher, a company's
servers. Escudo points it **sideways** — from a neighbour, to the neighbours,
with nobody in the middle.

That rules out a lot:

- **No control room.** Whoever receives an alert is whoever might deal with it.
- **No subscription, no contract**, nothing that can be discontinued or repriced.
- **No village budget to start.** The
  [layer that exists today]({{< relref "/technology/telegram" >}}) costs nothing,
  on phones people already own.
- **No accounts, no app, no dashboard** — nothing to log into during the worst
  ten minutes of your year.

## Run by the people in it

A Red Escudo belongs to its residents. That's how British Neighbourhood Watch has
worked for forty years — supported by the police, never owned by them — and how
the Dutch neighbourhood groups work.

There's no head office, no approval to seek, no certification. A village that
wants one, has one.

## Alongside the authorities, never around them

Horizontal doesn't mean autonomous. Policing is a state function; organised
citizen patrols have no legal cover in Spain, and Escudo is not a patrol.

But it doesn't need clearance either, and asking for it would be a mistake.
Neighbours agreeing to help each other is not a policing activity, so there is no
authority whose job it is to approve it — and presenting it as though there were
invites a *no* from someone who was never asked to decide. **Escudo is set up by
the village, and the authorities meet it the ordinary way: by being called.**

What keeps that honest is behaviour, not paperwork. Every alert carries **062**
and **112** on its face. Nobody patrols, confronts or detains. Nothing is
filtered before it reaches the Guardia Civil — a Red Escudo is a village that
calls them faster and with better information, never a layer in front of them.

The [Junta Vecinal and the Ayuntamiento]({{< relref "/protocols/introducing-the-scheme" >}})
are a different matter: they're the village's own institutions, they may put up
the sign, and they should hear about it from you.

## Down into the software

The bot is open source, configured per village in one file, and runs on free
hosting or a machine in someone's house. There's no central database of Spanish
villages, because there's no reason for one to exist.

If this project disappears tomorrow, a village that adopted it keeps the code,
the artwork, the runbook — and the network of people.
